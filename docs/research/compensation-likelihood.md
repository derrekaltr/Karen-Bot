# Corporate Service-Failure Compensation Research Report

**Market:** United States\
**Date:** October 2026

## Executive Findings

This report focuses on companies and industries known to provide
compensation, refunds, credits, vouchers, points, reimbursements, or
other remedies when customers experience legitimate service failures.

There are three main categories:

1.  **Contractual or automatic compensation** --- compensation is
    defined by an SLA, guarantee, or published policy.
2.  **Established refund/credit workflows** --- customers can request
    compensation for documented service failures.
3.  **Discretionary goodwill compensation** --- representatives may
    issue credits, points, vouchers, fee waivers, or refunds even when
    not strictly required.

The first two categories are generally the most predictable and
scalable.

# 1. Airlines

Airlines are particularly notable because the U.S. Department of
Transportation maintains a dashboard documenting commitments made by
major airlines for controllable cancellations and delays.

**Reference:** U.S. DOT Airline Cancellation & Delay Dashboard\
https://www.transportation.gov/airconsumer/airline-cancellation-delay-dashboard

Major carriers covered include Alaska, Allegiant, American, Delta,
Frontier, Hawaiian, JetBlue, Southwest, and United.

For qualifying controllable disruptions, remedies can include:

-   Free rebooking
-   Meal assistance
-   Hotel accommodations
-   Ground transportation
-   Travel credits or vouchers
-   Refunds
-   Miles/points

### JetBlue

JetBlue has a relatively explicit passenger-compensation structure.
Qualifying passengers can receive compensation following certain
disruptions, and qualifying compensation for direct bookings may be
issued automatically. Customer Bill of Rights compensation is generally
issued as TrueBlue points under the current policy.

Potential qualifying scenarios include:

-   Delayed flights
-   Cancelled flights
-   Major schedule changes
-   Downgrades from Mint
-   Misconnections
-   Baggage issues
-   Paid ancillary services not provided

**Rating:** 9.5/10

References:

-   https://www.jetblue.com/help/refunds
-   https://www.transportation.gov/airconsumer/airline-cancellation-delay-dashboard

### Southwest Airlines

For qualifying controllable delays of three or more hours, remedies can
include:

-   Meal vouchers/reimbursement
-   Hotel accommodations
-   Ground transportation
-   Travel credits

Southwest also provides a reimbursement process for certain reasonable
expenses incurred by affected passengers.

**Rating:** 9/10

Reference:

-   https://support.southwest.com/helpcenter/article/delayed-or-canceled-flight

### Delta Air Lines

Delta's Customer Service Plan provides representatives discretion to
issue compensation for passenger inconvenience.

Potential compensation can include:

-   Gift cards/cash equivalents
-   Travel credits
-   Vouchers
-   SkyMiles
-   Meals
-   Hotels
-   Ground transportation
-   Reimbursement for certain reasonable expenses

**Rating:** 9/10

Reference:

-   https://www.delta.com/us/en/legal/customer-commitment

# 2. Telecom / Internet

Telecom is an attractive category because network outages are often
electronically documented by the provider, reducing ambiguity about
whether a service failure occurred.

## AT&T

The AT&T Guarantee provides credits for qualifying service
interruptions.

Examples include:

-   Qualifying AT&T Fiber/Internet Air interruptions of 20+ minutes: a
    full day of service credit.
-   Qualifying wireless outages of 60+ minutes that satisfy AT&T's
    network-impact criteria: a full day of service credit.
-   Eligible network-outage credits may be issued automatically.

**Rating:** 10/10

Reference:

-   https://www.att.com/why-att/guarantee/

## Xfinity / Comcast

Xfinity provides an online process for customers affected by certain
outages to check eligibility for account credits.

Its automated support systems can also process certain credits
involving:

-   Service outages
-   Installation fees
-   In-home service visit fees
-   Early termination fees
-   Video/rental purchases

**Rating:** 10/10

References:

-   https://www.xfinity.com/support/articles/check-service-outage
-   https://www.xfinity.com/support/articles/credit-xfinity-assistant

# 3. Hotels

Hotels can offer significant compensation when a confirmed reservation
cannot be honored, particularly for eligible loyalty-program members.

## Marriott Bonvoy

Marriott's **Ultimate Reservation Guarantee** protects qualifying elite
members when a participating hotel cannot honor a confirmed reservation.

Depending on the brand and status, remedies may include:

-   Comparable nearby accommodation paid by the hotel
-   Cash compensation
-   Marriott Bonvoy points

At certain participating brands, compensation can reach:

**\$200 + 90,000 Marriott Bonvoy points**

Marriott also maintains an **Elite Benefits Guarantee** covering certain
promised elite benefits that are not delivered, with cash compensation
varying by brand and circumstance.

**Rating:** 10/10 for eligible customers

References:

-   https://help.marriott.com/s/article/ultimate-reservation-guarantee
-   https://www.marriott.com/loyalty/terms/default.mi

# 5. B2B SaaS / Cloud Services

This is one of the most significant categories.

Businesses may spend hundreds, thousands, or tens of thousands of
dollars each month on cloud infrastructure and recurring software. Many
providers contractually guarantee a specific level of uptime through a
Service Level Agreement (SLA).

If uptime falls below the contractual target, the customer may become
entitled to service credits.

## Amazon Web Services (AWS)

Numerous AWS products have financially backed SLAs.

A representative SLA structure used by certain AWS services is:

  Monthly Uptime   Potential Service Credit
  ---------------- --------------------------
  99.0%--99.9%     10%
  95%--99%         25%
  Below 95%        100%

Customers generally must submit a claim and provide required information
or supporting evidence.

**Rating:** 10/10 for B2B

Example reference:

-   https://aws.amazon.com/security-hub/sla/

## Google Cloud

Google Cloud maintains financially backed SLAs across various services.

An example structure for a covered service:

  Uptime       Potential Credit
  ------------ ------------------
  99%--99.9%   10%
  95%--99%     25%
  Below 95%    50%

Terms and thresholds vary by product.

**Rating:** 9.5/10

Example reference:

-   https://cloud.google.com/application-integration/sla

## Atlassian

Certain Atlassian Premium cloud products carry a 99.9% SLA, while
certain Enterprise products carry a 99.95% SLA.

An example Premium credit structure:

  Uptime       Potential Credit
  ------------ ------------------
  99%--99.9%   10%
  95%--99%     25%
  Below 95%    50%

Claims are subject to eligibility requirements and submission deadlines.

**Rating:** 10/10

References:

-   https://www.atlassian.com/legal/sla
-   https://support.atlassian.com/subscriptions-and-billing/docs/service-level-agreement-for-atlassian-cloud-products/

## Cloudflare

Cloudflare Enterprise offers contractual service-level commitments,
including a 100% uptime commitment for applicable services under its
Enterprise SLA.

Qualifying SLA failures may result in service credits. Contractual
limits and eligibility conditions apply.

**Rating:** 9.5/10

Reference:

-   https://www.cloudflare.com/enterprise-support-sla/

# Strategic Opportunity: Unclaimed Contractual Service Credits

One of the most commercially interesting findings is the potential
market for identifying and recovering **unclaimed contractual service
credits**.

Consider a business spending:

-   **\$20,000/month** on a cloud/software service
-   Contractual uptime guarantee: **99.9%**
-   Actual qualifying uptime: **98.8%**
-   Applicable SLA credit: **25%**

Potential service credit:

**\$5,000**

The customer may still need to:

1.  Detect the SLA breach.
2.  Determine whether the incident qualifies.
3.  Collect logs/evidence.
4.  Calculate the potential credit.
5.  File the claim within the required deadline.
6.  Follow up with the vendor.

If nobody performs these steps, the business may leave a legitimate
contractual credit unclaimed.

This suggests a potential contingency-based service:

> Connect or review a company's SaaS/cloud services, identify qualifying
> SLA failures, document the claim, assist with submission, and charge a
> percentage of successfully recovered credits.

Any such service would need to comply with applicable contracts, laws,
authorization requirements, vendor terms, and rules concerning
representation or claims handling.

# Compliance Note

This research concerns compensation for **legitimate, documented service
failures**.

There is a significant distinction between recovering compensation a
customer is contractually or legitimately entitled to and manufacturing
or falsifying complaints. False outage claims, fabricated missing-item
claims, fake hotel problems, or other intentionally false
representations may constitute fraud and can also result in customer
accounts being restricted or terminated.

The strongest potential business opportunities identified here rely on
objectively verifiable incidents such as network outages, airline
disruptions, reservation failures, and contractual SaaS uptime breaches.
